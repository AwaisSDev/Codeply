About

AI that’s around you

Yune works where you are, keeps going when you leave, and asks when it needs you.

The problem

Everyone has the same models now. What’s missing is where they live.

Most AI waits in a tab. You copy your work into it, explain what you’re doing, paste the answer back and check whether it broke anything. The moment you close the laptop, it stops. The intelligence is there. It’s just in the wrong place.

What “around you” means

  • At your desk. Yune edits your project, runs it and checks the result in a real browser before it says it’s done.
  • In whatever app you’re in. Press Alt + C and Yune drops into the corner of your screen.
  • In your terminal. Yune Code is the same agent, one line to install.
  • When you leave. Send it to the cloud and the task keeps going while your PC is off, then merges when it’s done.

How we build it

  • It checks its own work. Yune opens what it changed and reads back real errors, not a guess.
  • Nothing without your yes. Every write, edit and command asks first, and a whole message can be undone in one click.
  • Your models, or ours. Yune picks the right model for each step, or you bring your own keys and local models.
  • Free to start.

Who builds it

Awais SiddiqueFounder and developer · Multan, Pakistan

Yune is built by Awais, a self-taught developer who has been coding since he was 10. Python, TypeScript, JavaScript, C++ and frameworks like React, Laravel and Electron came one project at a time, and curiosity turned into shipping full products: AI platforms, desktop apps, browser tools and machine learning research.

Yune started as Codeply, a desktop app for placing AI-written code in the right file, with Windows and Mac builds, Supabase sign-in and a custom search-and-replace pipeline for precise edits. It has since grown into Yune and Yune Code. Alongside it he builds Flaren, an autonomous AI marketing platform, and Tracyn, which records what AI agents do and turns it into evidence for audits. He is also validating the market for Dovetto, a shared intelligence layer for sales teams, and works on research like ICU deterioration detection and flood-response graph neural networks for Pakistan.

He likes problems that are properly hard, and turning them into things people can actually use.
